# THE RED LINE

**Status:** v2 — the story root · 2026-08-01
**Audience:** the Game Master. Never shipped to players as content.
**Public repo caveat:** nothing here is secret, it is *unpublished*. GM-facing
means "not told yet", not "hidden from anyone who looks".

---

## 0. The tree

This document is the root. It is no longer a physics paper — it is **a story**,
with people in it, and the twists in it are the ones the whole project spends
years walking players toward. The chapters are branches: each breaks a piece of
the root down for players and may add its own layer of drama. Every entity —
monster, item, NPC, scenery — is a leaf: it either tells a small true story of
its own life, or it hands the reader one puzzle piece of the root, and the best
leaves do both.

What is written here **does not change**. Cut it and everything downstream is
dead wood. Grow it — new people, new wounds, new ages — as much as you like.

`lore/revelations.json` tracks, beat by beat, how much of this root has reached
players: **hidden → hinted → revealed**, with pointers to the exact texts that
do the telling. The build refuses a beat that lies about its own status.

---

## 1. The premise

**Nangijala is the country on the far side of death, and it is made of what the
dead still remember.** What is spoken of holds its shape. What no one speaks of
goes soft, then strange, then gone. Nothing here can die — but everything here
can be forgotten, and forgetting is worse, because it happens while you watch.

Under that premise, one story, in four movements: a woman who learned the
world's one rule by losing her son to it; a man who loved his wife so hard he
became the worst thing in the world; a scribe who found out what leaving takes
with it; and the tired handful keeping one fire lit on a meadow, now, while the
rest of the map goes quiet around them.

---

## 2. THE LAWS (physics, compressed)

1. **The Waking.** The dead arrive by waking on the meadow, always the meadow,
   within sight of the stone house and the fire. You bring what you wore and
   what you can still remember; most cannot say their own name for a while.
   **Not everyone who dies wakes here. Some never come.** No one knows why, and
   that single fact does more damage in this story than every monster combined.
2. **The Keeping.** What is remembered holds. What is *told aloud, to somebody
   listening*, holds far longer. Every era's dead are here at once, so every
   era's world is: an aqueduct, a windmill, telegraph poles in one field —
   each one somebody's certainty, standing until the certainty is no longer
   spoken of.
3. **The Quiet.** You fade in four stages — Bright, Faded, Habit, Quiet. It is
   dementia, seen from outside and slowed to the pace of centuries: first the
   details, then the person, then a behaviour walking a round with nobody home,
   then nothing. Most wild creatures are stage three. Being broken in a fight
   is not this — you re-form beside the nearest tended fire, minus a little of
   what you were thinking.
4. **The Drift.** The land is held by the same imperfect remembering as
   everything else, so it moves. An episode is called a Turn. (Engineering
   note: the world map regenerates from rules; this law makes churn canon.)
5. **The Hard Pieces.** Thin far enough and one thing refuses to thin further:
   a soulstone — one fact, one appetite, one habit of being, pressed to
   mineral. One kind of creature, one kind of stone, no exceptions. Except the
   masks. See movement III.

*(The shooting star on player join is a leftover mechanic the maintainer
intends to remove. It is NOT canon. Nothing below references stars.)*

## 3. THE STORY

### Movement I — Signe and Emil (how the world learned its rule)

Signe woke early in the world's history with her boy **Emil** already there
waiting — he had gone through the ice at seven, she had followed years later,
and the first thing Nangijala ever gave her was the sound of him laughing on
the far side of a meadow. The country was young and crowded with wonders, and
nobody yet knew it needed maintenance.

Signe was not a talker. That is the whole tragedy in one line. She loved
enormously and said almost none of it, on the old reasoning that what is said
gets smaller. When Emil began to fade — slower to name things, then slower to
be named — she did what silent people do: she gripped harder and said less,
because saying it aloud would make it real.

He went Quiet in front of her. Not dramatically. He was holding her hand, and
then the hand was empty, and the place he had been was slightly wider than it
should have been. **He died twice, and the second time was her fault, and the
fault was silence.** She spent a season not speaking at all — and watched, with
a scholar's horror, how fast everything around her went soft while she did.
That was the discovery. Not an insight — an autopsy. *The things people talk
about stay.* She lit a fire, because you cannot make people sit and listen in
the dark and the cold, and she told a stranger about Emil until she could no
longer speak, and the patch of world around that fire firmed up like ground
after frost.

Everything since — the fires, the rota, the courtesy of sitting with the newly
woken and asking their name and one true thing — is Signe's apology to a boy
who cannot hear it. She never forgave herself, and she never went on, and late
in her age people say she confused the two: staying had *become* the apology.
What became of her is not written. (Beat B10 holds the option that she is
still somewhere, at stage Habit, tending a fire that isn't there.)

### Movement II — Valter and Liv (the hole under the mountain)

**This is the twist the whole setting is built around. Guard it.**

Valter was the first meeting-man — the founder of the other half of the
courtesy. Where Signe kept the fires, Valter walked to the wakings. For over a
century, whoever opened their eyes on that meadow saw the same thing first: a
big, plain, patient man sitting a little way off, who told you gently what had
happened to you, asked your name, and stayed until you could stand. Thousands
of the dead were carried through their worst hour by him. He was, by every
account that survives, **the kindest person Nangijala ever produced.**

He was waiting for his wife. **Liv.** He had died first, as husbands often do,
and he met every waking for a hundred years because one of them was going to
be her. He learned faces the way other people learn songs. And she did not
come. And she did not come. And she did not come.

Do the arithmetic he did, because he did it every day: she was younger than
him, but not *that* much younger. Forty more years, at the outside. Then
surely. Then long past surely. Every possibility he could imagine was
unbearable: that she had woken elsewhere (there is no elsewhere); that she was
somehow lost between; that something had *taken* her on the way. The one
possibility he could not imagine was the true one — and the truth is the
cruelest sentence in this document:

**Liv is not dead.** She simply lived. She grieved him, and recovered, as the
living do, and grew old very slowly, and is alive still. The dead get no news
of the living — that is the one mercy Nangijala refuses everyone — and so the
kindest man in the world concluded, from a century of silence, that his wife
had been stolen. And he went under the eastern mountain to find the thief.

What is under the mountain now is what a century of that makes. Valter began
*taking* memory — from the ground, from creatures, from anyone who came close —
because with enough of other people's remembering he could build her from
description. He has her laugh from somebody who heard it once at a market. He
has her hands from a glovemaker. **The masks are his failures.** Every
Hollowed creature is a vessel he emptied to make room for a draft of her, and
every draft collapses — because a person assembled from other people's
memories is exactly what this world's physics cannot hold, there being no one
inside — and what survives of each attempt is the face. Smiling. **Because in
every memory anyone ever shared of her, Liv was smiling.** He cannot get the
smile wrong. It is the only part of her he is sure of, and the mountain is
slowly filling with it.

He is not malevolent. He is not even angry. He is a man who has been standing
at a door for three hundred years, and the hunger the cave radiates is just
what waiting turns into when it stops being able to end. The GM should hold
this line hard: **the antagonist is the best of them, continued past the point
where love has anything left to be.**

Far-future arc, locked now: one day Liv dies — old, full of years, having
loved again, which he will have to hear — and wakes on the meadow like
everyone else. What happens then is the endgame of the entire game, and it is
not written here on purpose, except for one promise: it will not be a battle
that solves it.

### Movement III — Bettany and Britt (what leaving takes)

The dead can leave. Remember yourself completely — whole, needing no one to
hold any piece of you — and one evening you are simply not in the room, and
the northern sky lights up. The tellers call it the Long Morning.

**Bettany** is a scribe. Her sister **Britt** went on, young in death and
bright as a coin, and Bettany prepared herself for the grief the way you
brace for cold water. It never came. She remembers Britt perfectly —
every year, every fight, the exact pitch of her singing — and can find no
sorrow anywhere in herself, and *that* is what broke her. Grief was the last
thing of her sister she owned, and something took it. Everyone bereaved by the
Long Morning reports the same absence, and everyone calls it a mercy, and
Bettany started the Chronicle — the book the players read; the wiki is her
book — because she refuses to call it that until she knows **what it is**.

The truth (beat B9, hidden): going on is real, and it is not theft — it is
weight-bearing. The one who leaves gathers up, on the way out, every grief
that others were carrying *for them*, and takes it along, so that staying
behind costs the left-behind nothing. It is the gentlest thing in the whole
cosmology and it is experienced, from below, as a violation. When this is
finally revealed, it should re-colour every earlier scene of missing grief:
Britt did not take Bettany's sorrow from her. **She carried it out, because
that is what going on is — the last load you lift.**

The dark question this movement exists to hold (never resolve cheaply): the
way out is open to everyone, costs nothing, and almost no one takes it. A
world of the dead, each with their reason to stay — waiting, watching, owed,
working, refusing. The leap that ends *The Brothers Lionheart* lives inside
this question, and this game answers it with a whole cast instead of once.

### Movement IV — Now (the Thinning)

The rota is a fiction maintained by people who will not admit it lapsed. The
fires are down to a handful. And on the meadow where everyone wakes, the last
one burning is kept by **Solveig** — a quiet, stubborn woman who took the
watch over from someone she loved and lost to it (her predecessor thinned at
the fire, mid-vigil, and Solveig finished the shift before she cried). She
could go on any evening she chose. She has the reason everyone at a fire has:
*if I go, who meets them?* She is the §8 rule made flesh, and the first NPC a
player should ever love.

Around her, the present cast (all real characters in `characters2/`, linkable
in text): **Bettany** writing the Chronicle and interviewing everyone who will
sit; **Sigrun**, weaponsmith, who cracked the shape of her own thinned husband
**Stig** rather than let a stranger do it, wears his stone in her hammer's
head, and has not said his name aloud since (the trade's two words,
*releasing* and *cracking*, are her biography); **Wendell**, an elder who was
there when the east road was still safe and will trade what he remembers of it
for company; **Osric**, a blacksmith who arrived only recently and still
counts in living years; **Jehanne**, a priestess of a faith she has stopped
believing and cannot stop practising, because the practice is a fire rota and
people need the fire even if the prayer is to no one; **Gunnar**, a wanderer
mapping the Drift, who has watched the soft ground move toward the meadow and
keeps his measurements to himself; **Hearn**, a veteran of the one organised
attempt anyone ever made on the cave, who came back alone and unhollowed and
will not say a word about the inside except that "it asked politely."

These people know each other. They argue, owe each other, avoid each other.
Every leaf written from here on should stand *in* this web — name a neighbour,
carry a grudge, want something from somebody — because relationships are what
the maintainer correctly called the missing organ of the first draft.

## 4. THE BEATS

The trackable units of the root, in `lore/revelations.json` with per-beat
status and pointers. Summary of intent: what players can know now is the
world's *rules* and its *sorrows* (Signe, the stages, the stones, missing
grief as a phenomenon); what stays back is the *identity* chain — person →
meeting-man → Valter → Liv → Liv alive — which is years of play, revealed in
that order. Never spend two identity beats in the same season.

## 6. HOUSE RULES, v2

1. **Something must happen.** Every text names a person, a want, a loss, or a
   betrayal — atmosphere is seasoning, never the meal.
2. **Short unless it earns it.** Entities: ~200 words typical, 425 hard cap
   (enforced). Chapters may run long only while they are pulling.
3. **Link the names.** A name in prose is `[[domain/id|Name]]` when the page
   exists. The web is the product.
4. **Dare the dark.** Dying children, silent grief, mercy and its two words,
   the leap — these are the material, handled plainly, never gratuitously,
   never explained away.
5. **Leaves are free.** A leaf may tell a story that touches the root nowhere
   — a life, a joke, a heartbreak of its own. Better a moving orphan than a
   dutiful cross-reference.
6. **Spend twists slowly.** Check `revelations.json` before writing; a hidden
   beat stays hidden. Advancing one is a deliberate GM-visible act.
7. **Art wins**, ids are the keys, geography by relation only — unchanged.
