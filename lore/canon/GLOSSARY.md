# Glossary — the controlled vocabulary of Nangijala

Words the lore is allowed to use, with their exact meaning. If a word is not
here, it is not canon yet; add it here first, then use it.

Consistency of vocabulary is most of what makes a world feel like one world.
Two agents calling the same thing by two names does more damage than a plot
hole.

---

## The world

**Nangijala** — the country on the far side of death. The whole setting. Always
capitalised, never "the afterlife" in player-facing prose (the characters live
here; they don't call it that).

**the Long Morning** — the further shore beyond Nangijala, where the dead go
when they remember themselves completely. Nobody has come back from it. The
aurora is held to be its light seen from underneath. *In-world this is a
belief, not a fact — write it as one.*

**a Turn** — an episode of the land re-remembering itself: coastlines shifting,
a path going elsewhere, a hill that is no longer there. Not a disaster; weather
on a longer clock.

---

## Arrival

**the Waking** — how the dead arrive: you wake in the meadow grass, within
sight of the stone house and the fire. There is no gate, no door, no journey
anyone remembers. Everyone begins on that meadow.

**the woken** — those who have arrived. **a waking** — one arrival, the event.

**to meet a waking** — to sit with a newly woken soul, tell them what happened,
and ask their name and one true thing while somebody listens. The oldest
courtesy in the world; Solveig keeps it on the meadow now.

**the unwoken** — the dead who never arrive. People die together and one wakes
and the other never does, and no one knows why, and everyone is afraid that
somebody does. The heaviest word in the language; use it sparingly.

**DISOWNED — never use:** starfall, new star, wild star, "meet a star",
falling-star imagery for arrival. The shooting star on player join is a
leftover mechanic the maintainer intends to remove; it was never canon.

## Losing

**the Quiet** — the end state: a shape letting go and simply not being anywhere
any more. Not death. Worse and slower. **The Quiet, not death, is what this
world is afraid of.**

**to thin** — to progress toward the Quiet. Intransitive. *"He's thinning."*

**Bright / Faded / Habit / Quiet** — the four stages of thinning. See red line
§3, Law III. "Bright" is also used as plain praise: *a bright one*.

**to go Quiet** — to reach the end. Never "to die"; nobody in Nangijala dies,
they are already dead.

**to be broken**, or **to go down** — to be knocked past the point of holding a
shape. Violent, unpleasant, and not an ending: what is left re-forms beside the
nearest tended fire over some hours. Never call this dying, and never call it
respawning.

**Hollowed** — emptied all at once rather than thinned gradually. The fast way,
the wrong way, the cave's work. The only condition this setting is comfortable
calling *wrong*.

---

## Keeping

**the Keeping** — the practice, collectively, of holding the world in shape by
remembering it. Also Law II.

**a telling** — one occasion of saying a true thing aloud to somebody who is
listening. The unit of maintenance.

**a fire** — a place where tellings happen. Also literally a fire, always. The
two senses are not separable and prose should not try to separate them.

**a rota** — the schedule of who keeps which fire. That the rota has lapsed
almost everywhere is the quiet horror of the present age.

**the Fire-Keepers**, also **tellers** — those who keep it warm. Grow the story,
accept the drift.

**the Chroniclers** — those who write it down. Fix the story, accept that it
stops growing. **The in-game wiki is the Chronicle**; a player reading it is
reading their work.

## Things

**a soulstone** — the last hard piece of a thinned thing, left when its shape
is broken. Cannot be forgotten and therefore cannot decay. One kind per kind of
creature. **Always displayed to players as simply "Soulstone"** — never given
an individual proper name in prose.

**to release** — to break a thinned shape and take the stone out, said by
somebody who means well.

**to crack** — the same act, said among people who do it for a living. The gap
between the two words is the moral range of the profession.

**junk** — the leavings: teeth, hide, bone, feathers, cracked stone. Worthless
individually, collected obsessively, because a pile of leavings is a story with
its details still attached.

**gold** — the one substance every dead person from every era remembers
identically, and therefore the only thing in Nangijala that never drifts.
Money, by discovery rather than decree.

---

## Creatures

**the Come** — creatures that fell, having been alive somewhere. Mostly
animals. They thin like people do.

**the Made** — creatures that never fell, because they were never anywhere:
remembering that pooled with nothing to be, thickened, and got up.

**the Hollowed** — creatures that were emptied. Kept the outside, lost the
inside. The only ones written as frightening rather than sad.

**a mask** — what survives when a Hollowed thing is broken. They keep smiling
because a face is the easiest part of a person to remember and the last part to
go.

## Words we do NOT use

This list governs **lore-authored text only** — everything under `lore/`. It
has no authority over the other domains, and several of them already ship words
on it: `monsters/config/roster.json` says "Slay it" of Grinmask and "old
earth-magic" of Mosscairn, and `items/config/roster.json` has "undead go
blind". Under our own precedence rule their copy wins, and that is correct —
being consistent with ourselves matters, overwriting another agent's work does
not. Where a term keeps recurring in a domain, propose the swap on the board
and let them decide.


- **"the afterlife"** in player-facing prose — too clinical; the cast lives here
- **"soul"** as a countable object — a soulstone is a *piece* of one, and the
  distinction matters to Law V
- **"undead", "spirit", "ghost", "revenant"** — imported baggage; we have the
  Come, the Made and the Hollowed
- **"god", "gods", "divine"** — no theology exists in this setting yet, and its
  absence is more interesting than any pantheon would be
- **"magic"** as an explanation — the laws are physics here; if something works,
  it works because of the Keeping
- **"kill", "murder", "slay"** for creatures — *release*, *crack*, *break the
  shape*
- **"respawn", "level up", "loot"** — system words; never in prose
